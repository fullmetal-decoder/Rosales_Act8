🌐 Internet Safety Basics

The internet is an essential part of daily life, but it comes with risks. Understanding the fundamentals of online safety helps you avoid scams, protect your identity, and browse with confidence.

Think Before You Click

Unexpected links and attachments are a top way malware spreads.
Hover over links to check where they really lead before clicking.

Keep Software Updated

Updates often patch security vulnerabilities.
Enable automatic updates on your OS, browser, and apps.

Use Secure Connections

Look for "https://" and a padlock icon before entering sensitive information on a website.

Back Up Your Data

Regular backups protect you from ransomware, hardware failure, and accidental deletion.

🔑 Password & Account Security

  1. Use a unique password for every account, and never reuse them.
  2. Use a password manager to generate and store strong passwords.
  3. Enable two-factor authentication (2FA) wherever it's offered.
  4. Avoid using personal info (birthdays, pet names) in passwords.
  5. Change passwords immediately if a service you use reports a breach.

🎣 Recognizing Scams & Phishing

Scammers often impersonate trusted organizations to steal money or information. Watch for these warning signs:

Urgency & Pressure

"Act now or your account will be suspended" is a classic scare tactic used to stop you from thinking clearly.

Suspicious Sender

Check the actual email address, not just the display name.
Scammers often use lookalike domains.

Requests for Payment or Info

Legitimate companies rarely ask for passwords, gift cards, or wire transfers via email or text.

Too Good to Be True

Unexpected prizes, lottery winnings, or investment "guarantees" are almost always fraudulent.

⚠️ If you're unsure: contact the company directly using a phone number or website you find independently, not one provided in the suspicious message.

🕵️ Protecting Your Privacy

  • Review privacy settings on social media regularly. Defaults are often more open than you'd expect.
  • Limit how much personal information you share publicly.
  • Use a VPN on public Wi-Fi to encrypt your traffic.
  • Be cautious with app permissions. Only grant access that's actually needed.
  • Regularly review and delete old accounts you no longer use.

🧒 Keeping Kids Safe Online

Children face unique risks online, including inappropriate content, cyberbullying, and contact from strangers.

Open Communication

Encourage kids to tell a trusted adult if something online makes them uncomfortable.

Parental Controls

Use built-in device controls and app-level restrictions appropriate to your child's age.

Teach Critical Thinking

Help kids question who they're really talking to online.

Set Boundaries Together

Agree on screen time, appropriate sites, and what personal information should never be shared.

✅ Quick Do's & Don'ts

A fast summary of everything above. Green is what to do, red is what to avoid.

✅ Do

  • Check the sender before opening any message.
  • Turn on 2FA for your important accounts.
  • Keep your devices and apps updated.
  • Report scams to the platform or app they came from.

❌ Don't

  • Share passwords or one-time codes with anyone.
  • Click links in messages you weren't expecting.
  • Use the same password on more than one account.
  • Post your address, school, or phone number publicly.
